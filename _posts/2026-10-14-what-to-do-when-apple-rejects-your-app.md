## Is it normal for Apple to reject your app?

Yes. Very normal.

In its [2025 App Store Transparency Report](https://www.apple.com/legal/app-store/transparency/2025), Apple says it reviewed 9,100,620 app submissions and rejected 2,093,244 of them. That is about 23%, or roughly 1 in 4.

![Bar chart showing Apple reviewed 9.1 million app submissions in 2025 and rejected 2.1 million, about 1 in 4](/images/uploads/apple-rejects-your-app-chart.jpg)

Read that again. Every year, millions of submissions get sent back. Big companies get rejected. Funded startups get rejected. Founders with full engineering teams get rejected.

Here is the part most people never hear. On its [App Review page](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/), Apple says over 40% of unresolved issues come from 1 rule: guideline 2.1, called App Completeness. In plain words, Apple wants a finished app. No crashes, no dead buttons, no "coming soon" screens, and a way for the reviewer to log in and test everything.

That means many rejections come down to details. And details can be fixed.

## What to do when Apple rejects your app: 5 steps

When the red X shows up, your brain wants to spiral. Don't let it. Follow the steps.

### 1. Read the full message before you react

Open App Store Connect (Apple's website where you manage your app) and go to the App Review section for your app. Apple's message will name the guideline your app broke, usually as a number like 2.1 or 5.1.1.

Read it twice. Copy it into a doc. Send it to your developer. Do not guess what Apple meant. Work from what they actually wrote.

### 2. Look up the exact guideline

Every guideline number links back to Apple's [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Read that section in full, not just the line in your message.

This is where AI saves you hours. I paste the rejection message and the guideline into Claude and ask: "Explain this in plain English, and give me a checklist of everything in my app that could trigger it." In 2 minutes I have a clear fix list instead of a headache.

### 3. Fix the exact issue, then check the usual suspects

Fix what Apple named first. Then scan your app for the other common problems before you resubmit, so you don't get sent back for something new. The table below is your shortcut.

### 4. Reply or appeal if Apple got it wrong

Sometimes the reviewer simply missed something. You can reply to App Review right inside App Store Connect and explain, or answer their questions.

If you truly believe your app follows the rules, you can appeal to the App Review Board. Apple allows 1 appeal per submission, and you need to give specific reasons. Be calm, clear and factual, and stick to evidence.

### 5. Resubmit and track the timeline

Once the fix is in, resubmit. Then put the dates on your calendar: submitted, in review, decision. If you have a launch plan, update it with a realistic new date and share it with your team.

### What not to do after a rejection

A rejection is a moment where small mistakes can cost you weeks. Avoid these:

- **Don't resubmit the same build and hope for a different reviewer.** If nothing changed, the answer will not change either.
- **Don't argue in your reply.** Reviewers are people. Clear and kind gets you further than frustrated and defensive.
- **Don't rush 10 changes at once without testing.** Fixing 1 problem and creating 2 new ones is the fastest way to a second rejection.
- **Don't go silent.** Your team, your creators and your early users can handle a delay. What they can't handle is not knowing.

## The most common App Store rejection reasons (and quick fixes)

Apple lists these on its App Review page as the issues it sees most often. Here they are in plain English.

| Rejection reason | What it means | Quick fix |
|---|---|---|
| Crashes and bugs | The app froze or broke during review | Test every screen on a real iPhone before you submit |
| Broken links | A link goes nowhere or to an error page | Tap every link, including support and privacy links |
| Placeholder content | "Lorem ipsum," test text or empty screens | Replace every placeholder with real content |
| Incomplete information | The reviewer could not log in or test a feature | Give Apple a working demo account and clear notes |
| Privacy policy issues | Missing or unclear privacy policy | Link your privacy policy inside the app and on your listing |
| Unclear data access requests | The app asks for photos, contacts or camera without saying why | Write a clear reason for every permission request |
| Inaccurate screenshots | Store images do not match the real app | Use real screenshots of the current version |

If you only do 1 thing before resubmitting, do the demo account. A reviewer who cannot get in cannot approve you.

## How to keep your launch team excited while you wait

This is the part nobody teaches you. A rejection delays your app, and it also tests your team.

I have 30 creators waiting to post. They signed up because they believe in VIXA. Here is how I think about keeping them close, and it works for any founder with a launch team.

- **Tell them first.** Your team should never learn about a delay from a public post. Send a short, honest message: what happened, what you are doing, and when you will update them next.
- **Give them a job.** Waiting feels long when there is nothing to do. Ask creators to film teaser content, practice their hooks, or share what they wish they had known about dating.
- **Show the process.** Behind-the-scenes content performs. "We got rejected, here is what we are fixing" is a real story, and real stories build trust.
- **Keep your word on updates.** If you said you would update them Friday, update them Friday, even if the news is "still in review."

## Frequently asked questions

**How long does Apple take to review an app?**
Apple says it reviews at least 50% of submissions in under 24 hours and 90% in under 48 hours. Your first submission and any resubmission go through the same review.

**Can I appeal an App Store rejection?**
Yes. If you believe your app follows the guidelines, you can appeal to the App Review Board. Apple allows 1 appeal per submission, and you need to give specific reasons why your app complies.

**What is the most common reason apps get rejected?**
App Completeness, guideline 2.1. Apple says it causes over 40% of unresolved issues. Crashes, broken links, placeholder content and missing demo logins all fall under it.
