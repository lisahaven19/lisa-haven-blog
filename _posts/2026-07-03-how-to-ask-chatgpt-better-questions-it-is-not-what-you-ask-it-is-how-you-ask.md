If you've ever typed a question into ChatGPT and gotten back an answer that felt generic, flat or just plain wrong, the problem is almost never the tool. It's the question. Here is the short answer on how to ask ChatGPT a question: tell it who to be, give it your situation, say exactly what you want back, and set your limits. Learning to ask this way is the fastest way I know to turn artificial intelligence from a toy into a real business partner, and most people were never taught how. I use artificial intelligence every day to run 3 companies as a mother of 3, and I built an entire platform around one belief: what matters most is HOW you ask.

## Why Your ChatGPT Answers Feel Generic

ChatGPT and Claude respond to exactly what you give them. When you type "write me an Instagram caption," you're asking the tool to guess your brand, your audience, your tone and your goal all at once. It can't read your mind, so it averages everything out and hands you something that sounds like everybody else. Give it something vague and you'll get something vague back. Give it specifics and you'll get specifics. That's really all there is to it.

## How to Ask ChatGPT a Question: The 4 Ingredients

Every strong prompt I write has the same 4 ingredients. First, a role: tell the tool who to be, like "act as a retail buyer for a national beauty chain." Second, context: your situation, your audience and your goal. Third, format: spell out exactly what you want back, whether that's a table, a script or 3 headline options. Fourth, constraints: word count, tone and anything it should avoid.

When I negotiate with manufacturers for [Havenly](https://www.havenly.co), my Gen Z beauty brand, I never ask "how do I negotiate." I ask, "act as a sourcing expert who has negotiated with beauty manufacturers for 20 years, I am a founder ordering my first 10,000 units, give me the 5 leverage points I have and the exact language to use for each one." Same tool, and a completely different result.

## Before and After: A Real Example

Weak question: "Give me marketing ideas for my app."

Better question: "Act as a growth marketer who has launched consumer apps for women. My app helps women get clarity on confusing dating conversations. My audience is women ages 22 to 40 who live on TikTok and Instagram. Give me 5 launch ideas ranked by cost, with the first step for each one."

## How I ask AI now: I just talk to it

Here is my honest confession. These days I barely type prompts at all.

I am running 3 companies and raising 3 daughters, so I move fast. I jump from window to window, and I just talk to Claude, out loud, like I would talk to a business partner. Over time it has learned me. It knows how I think and what I usually need.

But even when I talk fast, I still use the 4 ingredients. They are just built into how I speak. My go-to move is 1 word: **assess.**

**How I used to ask:** "What does this mean?"

**How I ask now:** "Assess this conversation and these screenshots. Tell me what is really going on, what I might be missing, and give me 2 ways to reply. I will make the final call."

That 1 change does 3 things:

1. **It asks for judgment, not just information.** "Assess" tells the AI to think like an advisor, not a search engine.
2. **It asks for options.** 2 replies to choose from, not 1 answer to accept.
3. **It keeps me in charge.** AI gives me the assessment. I make the decision, and I make it much faster.

That is the real secret. AI does not make my decisions for me. It helps me make better ones, quicker. I use this every day for customer messages, creator questions, business choices and even my own texts.

Try it today. Pick 1 thing on your plate, paste it in and say: "Assess this and give me 2 options."

## Common Questions

**Do I need to write long questions for ChatGPT?** Not long, just specific. 2 to 4 clear sentences with a role, context, format and limits beat a vague one liner every time.

**Can I ask ChatGPT a follow up question?** Yes, and you should. Treat it like a conversation. Ask it to shorten the answer, change the tone or explain a step. It remembers what you said earlier in the same chat.

**Does this work in Claude too?** Yes. The same 4 ingredients work in Claude and in every other AI chat tool.
