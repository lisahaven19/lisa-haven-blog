If you have ever typed a question into ChatGPT and received an answer that felt generic, flat, or completely off, the problem is almost never the tool. It is the question. Learning how to ask ChatGPT better questions is the single fastest way to turn artificial intelligence from a toy into a business partner, and most people were never taught how to do it. I use artificial intelligence every single day to run three companies as a mother of 3, and here is the truth I built an entire platform around: it is not what you ask, it is HOW you ask.

## Why Your ChatGPT Answers Feel Generic

ChatGPT and Claude respond to exactly what you give them. When you type "write me an Instagram caption," you are asking the tool to guess your brand, your audience, your tone, and your goal all at once. It cannot read your mind, so it averages everything and hands you something that sounds like everyone else. Vague in, vague out. Specific in, specific out. That is the entire game.

## The Four Ingredients of a Better Question

Every high performing prompt contains the same four ingredients. First, a role: tell the tool who to be, such as "act as a retail buyer for a national beauty chain." Second, context: give it your situation, your audience, and your goal. Third, format: tell it exactly what the output should look like, whether that is a table, a script, or three headline options. Fourth, constraints: word count, tone, what to avoid.

When I negotiate with manufacturers for [Havenly](https://www.havenly.co), my Gen Z beauty brand, I never ask "how do I negotiate." I ask "act as a sourcing expert who has negotiated with beauty manufacturers for 20 years, I am a founder ordering my first 10,000 units, give me the five leverage points I have and the exact language to use for each one." Same tool. Completely different result.

## Before and After: A Real Example

Weak question: "Give me marketing ideas for my app."

Better question: "Act as a growth marketer who has launched consumer apps for women. My app helps women get clarity on confusing dating conversations. My audience is women ages 22 to 40 who live on TikTok and Instagram. Give me five launch ideas ranked by cost, with the first step for each one."

The second version took thirty extra seconds to write and saves hours of back and forth. That is the return on asking better.
