## What is a UGC creator program?

UGC means user-generated content. It is short video made by real people, not shiny ads made by a brand.

Picture a girl in her bedroom, talking to her phone about a confusing text from a guy. It feels real. So people stop and watch.

A UGC creator program is a team of creators who make those videos for your brand, again and again, on a set plan.

I am building mine for [VIXA](https://www.vixaapp.ai), my dating clarity app for women. The app is submitted to the App Store and waiting on approval.

## Why I started before launch

Most founders launch first and look for creators second. I flipped it.

Here is why. A brand new social media account cannot post on day 1 and go viral. It has to be "warmed up" first. That means using it like a normal person for about 2 weeks so the platform trusts it.

If I waited for launch day to find creators, I would lose those 2 weeks at the most important moment. So my creators are warming up right now. The day the app goes live, the videos go out.

As a mother of 3 building 3 companies, I do not have time to waste. Neither do you.

## 7 lessons for building a UGC creator program

### 1. Start before you are ready

You do not need a live product to recruit. You need a clear brand, a clear offer, and a date that is coming. Creators like getting in early. I call mine the founding wave, and that name alone gets replies.

### 2. Pay for results

My standard deal is simple. A small paid trial first. After that, creators earn money for every 1,000 views their videos get.

Some creators asked for a flat monthly fee. I held my line. When pay is tied to views, the creator and I want the same thing: videos people watch.

Keep 1 standard deal for everyone. It is fair, and it is easy to explain.

### 3. Creators do not like to read

My first guide was too long. Nobody read it.

Now every creator gets a 2 page guide with big text and pictures. My training videos are 2 to 3 minutes each. If your directions take more than 5 minutes to understand, they are too long.

### 4. Give them a home

My creators live in a private Discord group. Discord is a free group chat app. That is where they get the guide, ask questions, and meet each other.

I welcome each new creator with a short personal video. It takes me 2 minutes. It tells her a real person is behind this brand and she matters here.

### 5. Hire a creator manager early

I tried to answer every creator message myself. It ate my whole day.

So I hired a creator manager on [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com), a website where you can hire freelancers from anywhere in the world. Her name is Sofia. She is from Brazil, and she is great.

Sofia checks in with the creators every day. Her job is to be warm, be quick, and keep everyone moving. She is the best hire I have made for this program.

My tip: start with a paid trial and clear goals. You will know fast if the person is right.

### 6. Find the winning video first, then grow

My goal is 50 creators. But I am not rushing to 50.

First I want to find the video style that works. The hook that makes women stop scrolling. Once I know it, I will teach it to everyone and grow the team. Growing before you know what works just means paying for more videos that do not work.

### 7. Expect people to leave

Some creators say yes and then go quiet. Some join and leave a week later. That is normal. Do not take it personally. Keep recruiting every week so your team never runs dry.

## How I use AI to run my creator program

Here is the part that surprises people. The team behind this program is small: me, Sofia, and AI.

I use [Claude](https://claude.ai), an AI assistant, with its Chrome browser extension. An extension is a small add-on for your web browser. It lets Claude work on websites for me, the same way an assistant would.

I do not write code. I give it commands in plain words. Here are real ones I use:

* "Answer the new creator messages."
* "Remind the creators to send their account links."
* "Welcome the new creators and send them the guide."
* "Show me a list of every creator and what she still has to do."
* "Find more creators with strong views and invite them."

Here is what that takes off my plate.

**Questions.** Creators ask the same things again and again. How does pay work? When do I start? Claude answers with the rules I already set.

**Reminders.** Creators are busy. A friendly nudge keeps them on track, and I do not have to remember to send it.

**Guides.** I talk through what I want. Claude turns it into a short guide with big text and pictures.

**Tracking.** I have 1 live list of every creator, where she is, and what comes next.

**Decisions.** When I am not sure, I ask for an honest opinion. It once told me not to hire a numbers person yet, and that saved me money.

I follow 2 rules with AI:

1. **AI follows my rules. It does not make them.** Pay, special deals, and who joins the team are my calls.
2. **People still need people.** Sofia brings the warmth. AI brings the speed. You need both.

## What I would do differently

I promised you the corrections too. Here are 2.

**I changed the rules too many times.** In the first few weeks I changed the pay plan and the steps more than once. Each change confused people. My advice: set your rules, then lock them for 30 days.

**I did not have example videos ready.** The first thing every creator asks is, "Can I see an example?" Have 2 or 3 sample videos done before you invite anyone.

## 1 rule you cannot skip

If you pay a creator, she has to say so in her post. In the United States this is a rule from the Federal Trade Commission, the government office that protects shoppers. Put it in your guide in plain words. It protects her, and it protects your brand.
